3D block assembly & counting cubes Spatial

A target shape is built from identical unit cubes. Five options show different groups of smaller blocks — only one set has the right total number of cubes and the right shape when joined together.

  1. Count the total cubes in the target shape first.
  2. Rule out any option set whose block-pieces don't add up to that total.
  3. For what's left, mentally rotate the pieces — a block can be turned any way, it just can't change shape.
Don't be fooled by an option with the right number of blocks but the wrong width/height/depth — always sanity-check the overall outline shape too, not just the block count.

Nets — folding flat shapes into 3D Spatial

A net is a 2D shape that folds up into a 3D solid. For a cube net: opposite faces on the finished cube are always two squares apart in the net, never adjacent.

Opposite pairs once folded: 1↔6, 2↔4, 3↔5 — each pair sits two squares apart along the strip.

If a question shows painted/patterned faces on a net and asks which two end up opposite (or touching) once folded, physically trace the fold with your finger on the page — imagining it in your head is where mistakes creep in.

Combining shapes at matching edges Spatial

A base shape has two edges marked x and y. A small shape is added on at the matching-letter edge, and another is added or subtracted at the other. Line up the letters, then work out the resulting outline.

Do this one step at a time — first add/remove at x, redraw the shape in your head, then add/remove at y. Trying to do both at once is where errors happen.

2D plan views of a 3D shape Spatial

You're shown a 3D object and asked to pick its view from directly above, the side, or the front — like a floor plan or an architect's elevation.

A stepped block, viewed from three different sides — each view only shows the outline, not the depth behind it.

Spotting the identical (rotated/reflected) shape Pattern

One of five shapes is an exact match for the target — just rotated or mirrored. The other four differ in a small, deliberate way (a missing line, different shading, a stretched side).

Pick one distinctive feature of the target (e.g. "the tail points towards the biggest angle") and track only that feature across the five options — much faster than comparing the whole shape at once.
