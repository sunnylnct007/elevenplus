Maths

Multiple-choice, A–E

For each question, work out the answer and choose the correct letter.

  1. What is the value of the 8 in the number 58,204?
    A) 8 ones    B) 8 tens    C) 8 hundreds    D) 8 thousands    E) 8 ten-thousands
    Answer: D) 8 thousands
    In 58,204, the digits represent 5 ten-thousands, 8 thousands, 2 hundreds, 0 tens and 4 ones, so the 8 is worth 8000.
  2. What is 3,902 rounded to the nearest hundred?
    A) 3900    B) 3800    C) 4000    D) 3950    E) 3000
    Answer: A) 3900
    The tens digit is 0, which is less than 5, so we round down to 3900.
  3. What is the missing number in this sequence? 100, 91, 82, 73, ?
    A) 60    B) 62    C) 63    D) 64    E) 65
    Answer: D) 64
    Each term decreases by 9: 100, 91, 82, 73, 64.
  4. Find &frac27; of 63.
    A) 9    B) 14    C) 16    D) 18    E) 21
    Answer: D) 18
    &frac17; of 63 = 9, so &frac27; = 2 × 9 = 18.
  5. A toy costs £12.75. Ben pays with a £20 note. How much change does he get?
    A) £6.25    B) £7.15    C) £7.25    D) £7.75    E) £8.25
    Answer: C) £7.25
    £20.00 − £12.75 = £7.25.
  6. A bus leaves at 14:05 and the journey takes 48 minutes. What time does it arrive?
    A) 14:43    B) 14:48    C) 14:53    D) 15:03    E) 15:13
    Answer: C) 14:53
    14:05 + 48 minutes = 14:53.
  7. What is 25% of 96?
    A) 18    B) 20    C) 22    D) 24    E) 26
    Answer: D) 24
    25% is a quarter. 96 ÷ 4 = 24.
  8. Which of these shapes has exactly 5 sides?
    A) hexagon    B) pentagon    C) square    D) triangle    E) octagon
    Answer: B) pentagon
    "Penta" means five, so a pentagon has 5 sides.
  9. This table shows the number of pages Amy read each day:
    Monday24
    Tuesday31
    Wednesday18
    Thursday27
    How many pages did she read in total over the four days?
    A) 90    B) 95    C) 100    D) 105    E) 110
    Answer: C) 100
    24 + 31 + 18 + 27 = 100.
  10. 3c − 5 = 16. What is the value of c?
    A) 5    B) 6    C) 7    D) 8    E) 9
    Answer: C) 7
    3c = 16 + 5 = 21, so c = 21 ÷ 3 = 7.

English

Comprehension & SPaG
Part 1 — Comprehension

Read this passage carefully, then answer the questions:

Ellie had always been curious about the attic, but her parents kept the door locked, insisting it was far too dusty and dangerous to explore. One rainy Saturday afternoon, however, she found the key hidden inside an old teapot on the mantelpiece.

Heart pounding, she crept up the narrow stairs, the wooden steps creaking beneath her feet. Sunlight filtered through a small round window, illuminating stacks of forgotten boxes and furniture draped in white sheets. Ellie pulled back one of the sheets and gasped: underneath sat an antique wooden trunk, its brass hinges tarnished with age.

She knelt down and carefully lifted the heavy lid. Inside lay a collection of old photographs, a faded map, and a leather-bound journal with yellowed pages. As she flicked through the journal, she realised the handwriting matched her grandmother's. Each entry described a different country her grandmother had visited as a young woman — places Ellie had never heard mentioned at family dinners.

Near the back of the journal, a folded letter slipped out and landed by her knee. Ellie unfolded it slowly, her fingers trembling with excitement, and began to read the elegant, looping handwriting inside.
  1. Where did Ellie find the key to the attic?
    A) Under her pillow    B) Inside an old teapot    C) In her mother's pocket    D) Behind a picture frame    E) In the garden shed
    Answer: B
    "…she found the key hidden inside an old teapot on the mantelpiece."
  2. Why were Ellie's parents reluctant to let her into the attic?
    A) They said it was haunted    B) They said it was dusty and dangerous    C) It was being redecorated    D) It belonged to a neighbour    E) It had no light
    Answer: B
    "…her parents kept the door locked, insisting it was far too dusty and dangerous to explore."
  3. What object did Ellie find under the white sheet?
    A) A wardrobe    B) A wooden trunk    C) A rocking chair    D) A bookshelf    E) A mirror
    Answer: B
    "…underneath sat an antique wooden trunk, its brass hinges tarnished with age."
  4. How did Ellie know the journal belonged to her grandmother?
    A) Her name was on the cover    B) Her mother told her earlier    C) The handwriting matched her grandmother's    D) There was a photograph of her    E) The journal was dated recently
    Answer: C
    "…she realised the handwriting matched her grandmother's."
  5. The word "trembling" suggests Ellie's fingers were…
    A) steady    B) cold    C) shaking    D) sticky    E) strong
    Answer: C
    "Trembling" means shaking slightly, often from excitement or nerves.
Part 2 — Spelling

Each sentence below is split into groups A–D. Find the group containing a spelling mistake, or choose N if there is no mistake.

  1. The exhausted athleteA recieved a medalB for herC achievement.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: B
    "recieved" should be "received" — i before e except after c.
  2. Seperate instructionsA were given toB each ofC the teams.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: A
    "Seperate" should be "Separate".
  3. The librarian carefullyA arranged theB booksC alphabetically.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: N
    Every word in this sentence is spelled correctly.
  4. He was greatfulA for theB unexpected surpriseC party.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: A
    "greatful" should be "grateful".
Part 3 — Punctuation & Vocabulary
  1. Its raining heavilyA outside so weB cannot go toC the park.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: A
    It should be "It's raining" — an apostrophe is needed for the contraction of "it is".
  2. Although it wasA late, the childrenB kept playingC outside.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: N
    The comma correctly separates the introductory clause from the main clause. No mistake.
  3. Choose a synonym for ancient:
    A) modern    B) old    C) shiny    D) heavy    E) small
    Answer: B
    "Old" means much the same as "ancient".
  4. Choose an antonym for reluctant:
    A) willing    B) nervous    C) tired    D) curious    E) careful
    Answer: A
    "Willing" is the opposite of "reluctant".
Part 4 — Writing Task
Prompt: Imagine you are Ellie. Write the opening of the letter you find in the journal, written by your grandmother describing one of her adventures abroad.

Write 8–10 sentences. Try to use:
  • First person ("I") and past tense
  • At least three descriptive adjectives
  • One rhetorical question or exclamation
Example answer:
My dearest Eleanor, I wonder if you will ever read these pages and picture me as a young woman, standing on the deck of a creaking ship bound for distant shores! The market in Marrakesh was unlike anything I had ever seen — a dazzling, chaotic tangle of spice stalls, woven rugs and shouting traders. I remember the sharp scent of cinnamon and the golden light falling across the sandstone walls. Could there really be a whole world hidden beyond our quiet village? I was determined to find out.

Check your own writing for: consistent first-person past tense, vivid adjectives, and correctly punctuated exclamations or questions.

Verbal Reasoning

Word puzzles
Part 1 — Same Letter, Two Gaps

The same letter completes the word before the brackets and begins the word after the brackets — and the same rule applies to a second, unrelated pair of words. Find the one letter that works for both pairs.

Worked example
mea[ ? ]able      si[ ? ]op
Answer: t — "mea" + t = "meat", t + "able" = "table"; "si" + t = "sit", t + "op" = "top". The letter t works for both pairs.
  1. spo[ ? ]ool      ma[ ? ]op
    A) t    B) p    C) s    D) l    E) n
    Answer: A) t
    "spo" + t = "spot", t + "ool" = "tool"; "ma" + t = "mat", t + "op" = "top".
  2. bea[ ? ]ear      spi[ ? ]ame
    A) n    B) s    C) t    D) m    E) d
    Answer: A) n
    "bea" + n = "bean", n + "ear" = "near"; "spi" + n = "spin", n + "ame" = "name".
  3. extr[ ? ]nt      vis[ ? ]ble
    A) a    B) e    C) i    D) o    E) u
    Answer: A) a
    "extr" + a = "extra", a + "nt" = "ant"; "vis" + a = "visa", a + "ble" = "able".
Part 2 — Double Meaning

Find the one word that fits both pairs of meanings equally well.

Worked example
(permit allow)      (rent hire)
Answer: let — "let" can mean "to permit/allow" and also "to rent out (a property)".
  1. (steady firm)      (horse building)
    A) stable    B) study    C) house    D) solid    E) shed
    Answer: A) stable
    "Stable" means steady/firm, and a stable is also a building where horses are kept.
  2. (fasten knot)      (draw score)
    A) knot    B) tie    C) bind    D) link    E) score
    Answer: B) tie
    "Tie" means to fasten with a knot, and it also means a drawn/equal score in a game.
  3. (illumination bright)      (not heavy)
    A) bright    B) light    C) glow    D) sun    E) airy
    Answer: B) light
    "Light" means illumination/brightness, and it also means "not heavy".
Part 3 — Move a Letter

One letter can be moved from the first word to the second word to make two new words. The remaining letters must not be rearranged.

Worked example
chill  →  old
Answer: c — "chill" − c = "hill"; "old" + c (at the front) = "cold".
  1. crane  →  am
    A) c    B) r    C) a    D) n    E) e
    Answer: B) r
    "crane" − r = "cane"; "am" + r (at the front) = "ram". Both are real words.
  2. plank  →  an
    A) p    B) l    C) a    D) n    E) k
    Answer: A) p
    "plank" − p = "lank"; "an" + p (at the front) = "pan". Both are real words.
  3. charm  →  ape
    A) c    B) h    C) a    D) r    E) m
    Answer: A) c
    "charm" − c = "harm"; "ape" + c (at the front) = "cape". Both are real words.

Section 4 — Cracking the Code

Each shape has two code letters: one for the shape and one for its shading. Work out the code, then find the code for the test shape. Remember the code is different for each question.

Worked example
solid star = WA     outline circle = XB
Test shape:
●
Answer: XA — X is the code for a circle (from the second shape) and A is the code for solid shading (from the first shape).
  1. solid triangle = CN     outline square = DP
    Test shape:
    ▲
    A) CP    B) DN    C) CN    D) DP    E) PN
    Answer: A) CP
    C is the code for a triangle, and P is the code for outline shading. An outline triangle is therefore CP.
  2. solid hexagon = EQ     outline pentagon = FR
    Test shape:
    ⬟
    A) FQ    B) EQ    C) FR    D) ER    E) QF
    Answer: A) FQ
    F is the code for a pentagon, and Q is the code for solid shading. A solid pentagon is therefore FQ.
