Maths

~20 minutes
Part 1 — Data & Statistics Warm-up

Answer these quickly — mentally or with brief working:

  1. A class voted for their favourite fruit. The bar chart shows the results:
    What is the mode (most popular fruit)?
    Answer: Banana
    The mode is the value that appears most often. Banana has the highest number of votes (9), so it is the mode.
  2. The number of books borrowed by 7 pupils in a month were: 3, 7, 2, 5, 4, 8, 5. Find the median number of books borrowed.
    Answer: 5
    First put the numbers in order: 2, 3, 4, 5, 5, 7, 8. The median is the middle value — with 7 numbers, that is the 4th one, which is 5.
  3. The temperatures recorded on 5 days were 18°C, 20°C, 19°C, 22°C and 21°C. Find the mean temperature.
    Answer: 20°C
    Add the values: 18 + 20 + 19 + 22 + 21 = 100. Divide by how many days: 100 ÷ 5 = 20.
  4. In a pictogram showing books read this month, each 📖 symbol represents 4 books. The row for Year 6 shows 5 symbols. How many books did Year 6 read?
    Answer: 20 books
    Each symbol = 4 books, so 5 symbols = 5 × 4 = 20 books.
  5. A bar chart shows the number of pets owned by each class:
    Which class owns the most pets?
    Answer: 6C
    Reading the tallest bar/largest value in the table, 6C has 11 pets — more than any other class.
Part 2 — Data Handling Word Problems

Show clear working for each question:

  1. Four friends compared how many minutes they spent reading over the weekend:
    (minutes spent reading)
    What is the total reading time for all four friends?
    Answer: 160 minutes
    40 + 55 + 30 + 35 = 160 minutes.
  2. Using the same chart as question 6, what is the mean (average) reading time per friend?
    Answer: 40 minutes
    Total ÷ number of friends: 160 ÷ 4 = 40 minutes.
  3. A pictogram shows the number of cars parked outside a school, where each car symbol represents 10 cars:
    Answer: 95 cars
    Monday: 3 × 10 = 30. Tuesday: 4.5 × 10 = 45. Wednesday: 2 × 10 = 20. Total: 30 + 45 + 20 = 95 cars.
  4. The bar chart shows the number of visitors to a museum each day:
    What is the range of visitor numbers over the week?
    Answer: 65
    Range = highest value − lowest value: 160 − 95 = 65.
  5. The mean number of points scored by 5 quiz players was 8. Four of the players scored 6, 9, 7 and 10 points. How many points did the fifth player score?
    Answer: 8 points
    If the mean of 5 players is 8, the total must be 8 × 5 = 40. The first four players scored 6 + 9 + 7 + 10 = 32, so the fifth player scored 40 − 32 = 8.

English

~20–25 minutes
Part 1 — Figurative Language & Vocabulary
  1. Which sentence contains a simile?
    A) The wind howled like a wolf.    B) The wind howled through the trees.    C) The wind was very loud.
    Answer: A
    A simile compares two things using “like” or “as”. Here the wind is compared to a wolf using “like”.
  2. Which sentence is a metaphor?
    A) The playground was as noisy as a zoo.    B) The playground was a zoo.    C) The playground was noisy.
    Answer: B
    A metaphor says one thing is another, without using “like” or “as”. Calling the playground “a zoo” is a direct comparison.
  3. Which word best replaces “said” to show that a character spoke in a frightened way?
    A) whimpered    B) announced    C) chuckled
    Answer: A) whimpered
    “Whimpered” suggests a quiet, fearful tone, matching the mood described. “Announced” sounds confident and “chuckled” sounds happy, so neither fits.
  4. Which sentence uses personification?
    A) The stars twinkled brightly in the sky.    B) The stars winked playfully at the sleeping town.    C) The stars were very bright.
    Answer: B
    Personification gives human qualities to non-human things. Stars cannot really “wink playfully” — that is a human action given to the stars.
  5. Which phrase uses alliteration?
    A) The bright blue balloon bounced by.    B) The balloon floated slowly upward.    C) The red balloon was round.
    Answer: A
    Alliteration is the repetition of the same starting sound in nearby words. Notice “bright”, “blue”, “balloon” and “bounced” all start with “b”.
Part 2 — Comprehension

Read this passage carefully, then answer the questions:

My new school badge felt heavy on my blazer, as if it had been pinned there just to remind me how much I did not belong. I stood alone at the edge of the playground, watching clusters of children who already knew each other's names, their laughter rising and falling like a tide I could not join. My stomach twisted into a tight knot. Then a girl with a gap-toothed smile bounced over and asked if I wanted to play tag. For a moment I could only stare, too surprised to answer. When I finally nodded, something inside me loosened, like a fist slowly unclenching. We ran across the playground together, and for the first time that morning, the size of the building did not feel quite so frightening.
  1. How does the narrator feel at the start of the passage? Give one word and one piece of evidence.
    Example answer: Nervous / anxious.
    Evidence: “My stomach twisted into a tight knot” or “how much I did not belong”.
  2. Find and copy a simile from the passage that shows how the narrator's feelings changed.
    Answer: “like a fist slowly unclenching”
    This compares the narrator's feelings to a fist relaxing, using “like”.
  3. What does the word “loosened” suggest about how the narrator was feeling? Choose the best answer.
    A) More relaxed    B) More frightened    C) More confused
    Answer: A) More relaxed
    “Loosened” suggests tension easing away, so the narrator is starting to feel calmer.
  4. This passage is written in the first person. What effect does this have on the reader?
    Example answer: Writing in the first person lets the reader hear the narrator's own thoughts and feelings directly, which helps us understand and share their nervousness and relief.
  5. Have you ever felt nervous about doing something new? Describe the situation and how you felt, in 2–3 sentences.
    Example answer: On my first day at swimming club, I did not know anyone and felt shy about getting into the pool. My hands felt cold and my heart beat quickly, but once another swimmer smiled at me, I began to feel much braver.
Part 3 — Writing Task
Prompt: Imagine you are the narrator from the passage above. Write a diary entry for that evening, describing your first day at your new school and how your feelings changed.

Write 8–10 sentences. Try to use:
  • First-person language (I, my, me)
  • At least one simile or piece of personification
  • Words that show how your feelings changed during the day
Example answer:
Dear Diary, today was my first day at my new school and I felt sick with nerves before I had even walked through the gate. My badge felt heavy, like a weight pinned to my chest, reminding me that I was the only new face in the playground. At break time I stood alone, watching everyone else laugh together like old friends. Then a girl called Amelia asked if I wanted to play tag, and my whole body relaxed with relief. We ran around the playground until the bell rang, and for the first time all day I actually smiled. By home time, the school did not feel nearly as big or as scary as it had this morning. I think tomorrow might even be a good day.

Check your own writing for: consistent first-person voice, at least one simile or example of personification, clear time order (morning to evening), and a change in feelings shown through your word choices.
