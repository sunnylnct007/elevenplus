Maths

Multiple-choice, A–E

For each question, work out the answer and choose the correct letter.

  1. What is the value of the 6 in the number 4,682?
    A) 6 thousands    B) 6 hundreds    C) 6 tens    D) 6 ones    E) 6 thousandths
    Answer: B) 6 hundreds
    In 4,682, the digits represent 4 thousands, 6 hundreds, 8 tens and 2 ones, so the 6 is worth 600.
  2. Write "eight thousand and forty-seven" in figures.
    A) 8047    B) 8407    C) 847    D) 80047    E) 8470
    Answer: A) 8047
    Eight thousand = 8000, and forty-seven = 47, with no hundreds. 8000 + 47 = 8047.
  3. What is the missing number in this sequence? 5, 11, 17, 23, ?
    A) 27    B) 28    C) 29    D) 30    E) 35
    Answer: C) 29
    Each term increases by 6: 5, 11, 17, 23, 29.
  4. Find ⅗ of 40.
    A) 8    B) 15    C) 20    D) 24    E) 32
    Answer: D) 24
    ⅕ of 40 = 8, so ⅗ = 3 × 8 = 24.
  5. Pens cost 45p each. Freya buys 6 pens. How much change does she get from £5?
    A) £1.70    B) £2.20    C) £2.30    D) £2.70    E) £3.30
    Answer: C) £2.30
    6 × 45p = £2.70. Change from £5.00 = £5.00 − £2.70 = £2.30.
  6. A film starts at 18:40 and lasts 1 hour 55 minutes. What time does it end?
    A) 19:35    B) 20:25    C) 20:35    D) 20:45    E) 21:35
    Answer: C) 20:35
    18:40 + 1 hour = 19:40. 19:40 + 55 minutes = 20:35.
  7. What is 15% of £60?
    A) £4.50    B) £6    C) £8    D) £9    E) £15
    Answer: D) £9
    10% of £60 = £6. 5% = £3. 15% = £6 + £3 = £9.
  8. Which of these is NOT a polygon?
    A) triangle    B) circle    C) hexagon    D) pentagon    E) square
    Answer: B) circle
    A polygon is a flat shape with straight sides. A circle has a curved edge and no straight sides, so it isn't a polygon.
  9. This table shows sweets sold in a shop:
    Monday12
    Tuesday18
    Wednesday9
    Thursday15
    How many more sweets were sold on Tuesday than on Wednesday?
    A) 6    B) 7    C) 8    D) 9    E) 10
    Answer: D) 9
    18 (Tuesday) − 9 (Wednesday) = 9.
  10. b + 15 = 32. What is the value of b?
    A) 15    B) 17    C) 19    D) 47    E) −17
    Answer: B) 17
    32 − 15 = 17.

English

Comprehension & SPaG
Part 1 — Comprehension

Read this passage carefully, then answer the questions:

Mara had lived beside the old lighthouse for as long as she could remember. Every evening, she climbed the spiral staircase with her grandfather to light the great lamp before the sun disappeared beneath the waves. Tonight, however, storm clouds rolled in faster than usual, and the wind rattled the windows like an impatient visitor.

"The fishing boats are still out," her grandfather murmured, peering through the telescope. "They'll need every bit of light we can give them."

Mara's stomach tightened. Her father was one of the sailors due home that night, his small boat somewhere beyond the churning grey water. She climbed higher, her legs aching, until she reached the lamp room. The glass panes shuddered as rain lashed against them.

Together, they turned the great brass wheel that wound the mechanism, and slowly, the beam began to sweep across the darkening sea. Mara pressed her face to the cold glass, searching for any sign of a sail. Minutes passed like hours.

Then, far out on the water, a tiny light flickered in response — once, twice, three times. Her grandfather laughed with relief. "That's the signal, Mara. He's seen us. He's coming home."

Mara smiled, though her eyes stung with tears of relief, and stayed at the window until she was certain the little light was drawing nearer.
  1. Where does Mara live?
    A) On a fishing boat    B) Beside a lighthouse    C) In a harbour town    D) On an island    E) In a boat house
    Answer: B
    The passage opens: "Mara had lived beside the old lighthouse…"
  2. Why does the grandfather say the boats will need "every bit of light we can give them"?
    A) The lamp is broken    B) A storm has made it hard to see    C) It is a competition    D) The boats are lost forever    E) It is a festival
    Answer: B
    Storm clouds and rain are making conditions dangerous for the fishing boats still at sea.
  3. "Mara's stomach tightened" suggests she feels…
    A) hungry    B) excited    C) worried    D) angry    E) sleepy
    Answer: C
    This is a physical description of anxiety — she is worried about her father, who is out at sea.
  4. What do Mara and her grandfather turn to make the beam sweep across the sea?
    A) A telescope    B) A staircase    C) A brass wheel    D) A window    E) A sail
    Answer: C
    "Together, they turned the great brass wheel that wound the mechanism…"
  5. How does Mara know her father is safe?
    A) He telephones her    B) A boat sails into harbour    C) A light flickers back from the sea    D) Her grandfather tells her directly    E) The storm stops suddenly
    Answer: C
    "…a tiny light flickered in response — once, twice, three times" is the signal from her father's boat.
Part 2 — Spelling

Each sentence below is split into groups A–D. Find the group containing a spelling mistake, or choose N if there is no mistake.

  1. The nervous studentA recieved his examB results with aC shaky hand.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: B
    "recieved" breaks the "i before e except after c" rule — it should be "received".
  2. Her favourite hobbyA was definatelyB painting oldC bicycles.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: B
    "definately" should be "definitely".
  3. The committeeA anounced the winnerB during theC ceremony.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: B
    "anounced" needs a double n — "announced".
  4. The gardenerA planted seventeenB rows of tulipsC yesterday.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: N
    Every word in this sentence is spelled correctly.
Part 3 — Punctuation & Vocabulary
  1. The dogs bowlA was emptyB again thisC morning.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: A
    It should be "The dog's bowl" — an apostrophe is needed to show the bowl belongs to the dog.
  2. By the timeA we arrived, theB concert had alreadyC started.D
    A    B    C    D    N (no mistake)
    Answer: N
    The comma correctly separates the introductory clause from the main clause. No mistake.
  3. Choose a synonym for enormous:
    A) tiny    B) massive    C) narrow    D) quiet    E) distant
    Answer: B
    "Massive" means very large, like "enormous".
  4. Choose an antonym for generous:
    A) kind    B) selfish    C) wealthy    D) cheerful    E) polite
    Answer: B
    "Selfish" is the opposite of "generous".
Part 4 — Writing Task
Prompt: Imagine you are Mara. Write a diary entry describing the stormy night and how you felt when you saw the little light flicker back from the sea.

Write 8–10 sentences. Try to use:
  • Past tense throughout
  • At least three powerful adjectives or adverbs
  • One example of direct speech (with speech marks)
Example answer:
Dear Diary, tonight the storm was the fiercest I have ever seen. The wind screamed around the tower and rain hammered against the glass as Grandpa and I climbed to the lamp room. I could hardly bear to think about Dad's little boat out on that furious sea. "Keep watching," Grandpa said gently, so I pressed my face to the cold window. Then, at last, a tiny light blinked back at us from the darkness. I gasped with relief and hugged Grandpa tightly, tears streaming down my face.

Check your own writing for: consistent past tense, vivid adjectives/adverbs, and correctly punctuated speech.

Verbal Reasoning

Word puzzles
Part 1 — Move a Letter

One letter can be moved from the first word to the second word to make two new words. The remaining letters must not be rearranged. Find the letter that moves.

Worked example
chill  →  old     (remove a letter from "chill" and add it to "old")
Answer: c — "chill" − c = "hill"; "old" + c (at the front) = "cold". Both are real words.
  1. spare  →  our
    A) s    B) p    C) a    D) r    E) e
    Answer: A) s
    "spare" − s = "pare" (to trim); "our" + s (at the front) = "sour". Both are real words.
  2. beard  →  an
    A) b    B) e    C) a    D) r    E) d
    Answer: E) d
    "beard" − d = "bear"; "an" + d (at the end) = "and". Both are real words.
  3. trail  →  an
    A) t    B) r    C) a    D) i    E) l
    Answer: A) t
    "trail" − t = "rail"; "an" + t (at the end) = "ant". Both are real words.
Part 2 — Hidden Word

The first two letters of the first word combine with the first letter of the third word to make a short word in the brackets. Work out the rule, then find the missing word.

Worked example
(cabin [can] net) Answer: can — "ca" (first two letters of "cabin") + "n" (first letter of "net") = "can".
  1. (bacon [?] rope)
    A) bat    B) bar    C) ban    D) bag    E) bad
    Answer: B) bar
    "ba" (bacon) + "r" (rope) = "bar".
  2. (moist [?] wing)
    A) mob    B) mow    C) mop    D) mod    E) moo
    Answer: B) mow
    "mo" (moist) + "w" (wing) = "mow".
  3. (cargo [?] rain)
    A) cat    B) cab    C) can    D) car    E) cap
    Answer: D) car
    "ca" (cargo) + "r" (rain) = "car".
Part 3 — Letter Coding

Work out how the code has been made, then apply the same rule to a new word.

Worked example
If HAND is coded as IBOE, how would BIRD be coded?
Answer: CJSE — each letter of the code is one place after the real letter in the alphabet (H→I, A→B, N→O, D→E). Applying the same shift: B→C, I→J, R→S, D→E, giving CJSE.
  1. If LAMP is coded as MBNQ, how is DESK coded?
    A) EFTL    B) EFSL    C) DFTL    D) EGTL    E) EFTK
    Answer: A) EFTL
    Each letter shifts one place forward: D→E, E→F, S→T, K→L.
  2. If GOLD is coded as EMJB, how is SILK coded?
    A) QGJI    B) RHKJ    C) QGJH    D) PFIH    E) QHJI
    Answer: A) QGJI
    Each letter shifts two places back: S→Q, I→G, L→J, K→I.
  3. If PEN is coded as RGP, how is BOX coded?
    A) DQZ    B) DPZ    C) CQZ    D) DQY    E) EQZ
    Answer: A) DQZ
    Each letter shifts two places forward: B→D, O→Q, X→Z.
