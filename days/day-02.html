Maths

~20 minutes
Part 1 — Fractions & Decimals Warm-up

Answer these quickly — mentally or with brief working:

  1. Write ⅗ as a decimal.
    Answer: 0.6
    ⅗ means 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6.
  2. Which is larger: ⅔ or ⅝?
    Answer: ⅔
    Common denominator 24: ⅔ = ¹₀⁄₂₄, ⅝ = ¹⁵⁄₂₄. 16 > 15, so ⅔ is larger.
  3. Round 0.478 to 1 decimal place.
    Answer: 0.5
    The second decimal digit is 7 (≥5), so round the first digit up: 0.5.
  4. Add: 0.35 + 0.6
    Answer: 0.95
    Line up the decimal points: 0.35 + 0.60 = 0.95.
  5. Simplify &sup8;⁄₁₂ to its lowest terms.
    Answer: ⅔
    Divide top and bottom by their highest common factor, 4: 8÷4 = 2, 12÷4 = 3.
Part 2 — Multi-step Word Problems

Show clear working for each question:

  1. Priya has £15. She spends ⅖ of it on a book and £3 on a pen. How much does she have left?
    Answer: £6
    ⅖ of £15 = £6 spent on the book. £15 − £6 − £3 = £6 left.
  2. A recipe for 4 people needs 0.6 kg of flour. How much flour is needed for 10 people?
    Answer: 1.5 kg
    Flour per person: 0.6 ÷ 4 = 0.15 kg. For 10 people: 0.15 × 10 = 1.5 kg.
  3. A jug holds 1.8 litres. It is ¾ full. How many millilitres of water are in the jug?
    Answer: 1,350 ml
    1.8 litres = 1,800 ml. ¾ of 1,800 = 1,350 ml.
  4. Three friends share a bill of £27.60 equally. How much does each person pay?
    Answer: £9.20
    27.60 ÷ 3 = 9.20.
  5. A shop reduces a £40 jacket by ¼ in a sale, then adds £2 for a matching scarf. What is the final price?
    Answer: £32
    ¼ of £40 = £10 off, so the jacket is £30. Add £2 for the scarf: £32.

English

~20–25 minutes
Part 1 — Grammar & Punctuation
  1. Which word in this sentence is an adverb?
    The dog barked loudly at the postman.
    Answer: loudly
    Adverbs often describe how an action is done and often end in -ly.
  2. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence:
    A) Its raining, so bring your coat.    B) It's raining, so bring your coat.    C) Its' raining, so bring your coat.
    Answer: B
    “It's” is the contraction of “it is”. “Its” (no apostrophe) shows possession instead.
  3. Add the missing apostrophe:
    The childrens coats were hung on the pegs.
    Corrected: The children's coats were hung on the pegs.
    “Children” is already plural, so add ‘s to show possession.
  4. Which sentence uses a subordinating conjunction?
    A) I like tea and coffee.    B) Although it was raining, we went outside.    C) The cat sat on the mat.
    Answer: B
    “Although” joins a subordinate clause to the main clause, showing contrast.
  5. Identify the noun phrase in:
    The tall, old oak tree stood in the field.
    Answer: The tall, old oak tree
    A noun phrase is built around a noun (“tree”) with describing words in front of it.
Part 2 — Comprehension

Read this passage carefully, then answer the questions:

Recycling helps to protect the environment in several ways. When materials such as paper, glass and plastic are recycled, fewer raw materials need to be taken from the earth. This saves energy and reduces pollution. Many schools now run recycling schemes, encouraging pupils to sort their rubbish into different bins. Some schools have even started composting food waste, turning it into rich soil for the school garden instead of sending it to landfill.
  1. According to the passage, what happens when materials are recycled?
    Answer: Fewer raw materials need to be taken from the earth.
    This is stated directly in the first two sentences.
  2. What do some schools do with food waste?
    Answer: They compost it, turning it into soil for the school garden.
    See the final sentence of the passage.
  3. Find and copy one word that means the same as “dirtying” or “contamination”.
    Answer: pollution
  4. Is this passage mainly fact or opinion? Give one reason.
    Answer: Fact.
    It gives real information about recycling and school schemes rather than personal feelings.
  5. Why might sorting rubbish into different bins be useful? Explain in your own words.
    Example answer: Sorting rubbish makes it easier to recycle each material properly, so less ends up wasted in landfill.
Part 3 — Writing Task
Prompt: Write a short letter to your head teacher persuading them to start a new recycling or gardening scheme at your school.

Write 8–10 sentences. Try to use:
  • A clear opening that states your idea
  • Persuasive words (e.g. “imagine”, “surely”, “important”)
  • A short concluding sentence
Example answer:
Dear Mrs Patel, I am writing to suggest that our school starts a composting scheme. Imagine how much less rubbish we would send to landfill if every classroom had its own food-waste bin! Surely it is important for us to look after our planet while we are still young. The compost could even be used to grow vegetables in our school garden, which would teach everyone new skills. I hope you will consider this idea. Yours sincerely, a concerned pupil.

Check your own writing for: a clear opening idea, at least two persuasive phrases, correct letter punctuation, and a short closing sentence.
