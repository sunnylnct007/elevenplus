Sutton Grammar 11+ Daily Practice

Maths & English — about 40–45 minutes total

Maths ~20 min English ~20–25 min Try every question on your own first!

Maths

~20 minutes
Part 1 — Time & Unit Conversion Warm-up

Answer these quickly — mentally or with brief working:

  1. Write “twenty to five in the afternoon” using the 24-hour clock.
    Answer: 16:40
    “Twenty to five” is 4:40. Afternoon means add 12 hours: 4:40 + 12:00 = 16:40.
  2. Convert 2.5 kilometres into metres.
    Answer: 2,500 m
    1 km = 1,000 m, so 2.5 × 1,000 = 2,500 m.
  3. Convert 4,200 grams into kilograms.
    Answer: 4.2 kg
    1,000 g = 1 kg, so 4,200 ÷ 1,000 = 4.2 kg.
  4. Convert 3 litres into millilitres.
    Answer: 3,000 ml
    1 litre = 1,000 ml, so 3 × 1,000 = 3,000 ml.
  5. How many minutes are there between 9:05 and 9:47?
    Answer: 42 minutes
    47 − 5 = 42 minutes (both times are in the same hour, so just subtract).
Part 2 — Measurement & Time Word Problems

Show clear working for each question:

  1. A bus timetable shows a bus leaving at 13:20 and arriving at 14:05. How long is the journey?
    Answer: 45 minutes
    13:20 → 14:00 = 40 minutes; 14:00 → 14:05 = 5 minutes. Total: 40 + 5 = 45 minutes.
  2. A baker has 1.2 kg of flour. A loaf recipe uses 750 g. How much flour is left after making one loaf, in grams?
    Answer: 450 g
    1.2 kg = 1,200 g. 1,200 − 750 = 450 g left.
  3. Jess has a ribbon 1.5 m long and another piece 85 cm long. What is the total length in centimetres?
    Answer: 235 cm
    1.5 m = 150 cm. 150 + 85 = 235 cm.
  4. A train leaves the station at 08:45. The journey takes 1 hour 50 minutes. What time does it arrive?
    Answer: 10:35
    08:45 + 1 hour = 09:45. 09:45 + 50 minutes = 10:35.
  5. Sam walks 900 m to school every morning and 900 m home every afternoon. How far does he walk in total over a 5-day school week, in kilometres?
    Answer: 9 km
    Each day: 900 + 900 = 1,800 m. Over 5 days: 1,800 × 5 = 9,000 m = 9 km.

English

~20–25 minutes
Part 1 — Punctuation & Sentence Structure
  1. Add the missing commas:
    For the picnic we packed sandwiches apples crisps and lemonade.
    Corrected: For the picnic we packed sandwiches, apples, crisps and lemonade.
    Use a comma after each item in a list, except usually before the final “and”.
  2. Punctuate this direct speech correctly:
    the teacher said sit down quietly please
    Corrected: The teacher said, “Sit down quietly, please.”
    Capital letter to start; comma before the spoken words; speech marks around what is actually said; the spoken sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with its own punctuation inside the speech marks.
  3. Is this a compound or a complex sentence?
    We wanted to play outside, but it started to rain.
    Answer: Compound sentence
    It joins two equal main clauses with the coordinating conjunction “but”. A complex sentence would join a main clause to a subordinate clause (e.g. using “although” or “because”).
  4. Which sentence is complex?
    A) The dog barked and the cat ran away.    B) Because the dog barked, the cat ran away.    C) The dog barked; the cat ran away.
    Answer: B
    “Because the dog barked” is a subordinate clause attached to the main clause “the cat ran away”, which makes it a complex sentence.
  5. This is a run-on sentence. Correct it:
    The rain poured down the streets flooded people rushed to find shelter.
    Corrected: The rain poured down. The streets flooded, and people rushed to find shelter.
    A run-on sentence crams several ideas together with no punctuation. Split it into separate sentences, or join related ideas with a comma and a conjunction.
Part 2 — Comprehension

Read this passage carefully, then answer the questions:

Octopuses are remarkable creatures that live in oceans all around the world. Unlike most animals, an octopus has no bones at all, which allows it to squeeze its soft body through the tiniest of gaps — even a hole no bigger than a coin. Octopuses are also famous for changing colour in an instant. Special cells in their skin let them match the rocks, sand or coral around them, helping them hide from hungry predators such as sharks and eels. Perhaps most surprising of all is their intelligence. Scientists have watched octopuses open jars to reach food inside, and some have even been seen carrying coconut shells to use as portable shelters. With eight flexible arms lined with hundreds of sensitive suckers, the octopus is truly one of the ocean’s most extraordinary inhabitants.
  1. Why can an octopus squeeze through a hole no bigger than a coin?
    Answer: Because it has no bones at all.
    The passage states this directly: “an octopus has no bones at all, which allows it to squeeze its soft body through the tiniest of gaps.”
  2. What is this passage mainly about?
    Answer: The amazing and unusual abilities of octopuses (their boneless bodies, colour-changing skin, and intelligence).
    Each part of the passage describes a different remarkable feature of the animal.
  3. What does the word “predators” mean in the passage?
    Answer: Animals that hunt and eat other animals.
    The passage gives sharks and eels as examples of predators the octopus hides from.
  4. How does the writer organise the information in this passage?
    Answer: The writer describes one amazing feature of the octopus at a time, in separate sentences — first its boneless body, then its colour-changing skin, then its intelligence, ending with a summary sentence.
  5. Summarise in one sentence why the octopus is described as “extraordinary”.
    Example answer: The octopus is extraordinary because its boneless body, colour-changing skin and clever problem-solving make it unlike most other animals.
Part 3 — Writing Task
Prompt: Write a short informal report about an unusual animal that interests you (real or imaginary), explaining what makes it special and how it survives in its habitat.

Write 8–10 sentences. Try to use:
  • A clear opening sentence introducing the animal
  • At least three interesting facts
  • Topic-related vocabulary (e.g. habitat, survive, adapt)
Example answer:
The Arctic fox is a small, hardy animal that survives in one of the coldest habitats on Earth. Its thick white fur keeps it warm and also helps it hide from predators in the snow. In summer, its coat changes to a brownish-grey colour so it can blend in with the rocks and soil. Arctic foxes have short ears and a compact body shape, which helps them lose less heat in freezing temperatures. They are excellent hunters, often listening for small animals moving beneath the snow before pouncing to catch them. When food is scarce, an Arctic fox can survive by following polar bears and eating the leftovers from their kills. These clever adaptations show how well the Arctic fox has adapted to its harsh environment.

Check your own writing for: a clear introductory sentence, at least three separate facts, correct use of full stops and commas, and topic vocabulary such as habitat, survive or adapt.
