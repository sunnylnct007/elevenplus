Maths

~20 minutes
Part 1 — Algebra & Reasoning Warm-up

Answer these quickly — mentally or with brief working:

  1. Find the missing number: 7 + ? = 15
    Answer: 8
    Work backwards: 15 − 7 = 8, so the missing number is 8.
  2. Find the next number in the sequence: 3, 6, 9, 12, ?
    Answer: 15
    Each term increases by 3, so 12 + 3 = 15.
  3. I think of a number, double it, then add 5. My answer is 23. What was my number?
    Answer: 9
    Work backwards: 23 − 5 = 18, then 18 ÷ 2 = 9.
  4. Find the missing number: ? × 4 = 32
    Answer: 8
    32 ÷ 4 = 8, so the missing number is 8.
  5. A function machine adds 7 to a number, then doubles the result. The output is 26. What was the input?
    Answer: 6
    Work backwards: 26 ÷ 2 = 13, then 13 − 7 = 6.
Part 2 — Week 1 Mixed Review

Each question combines two topics from this week. Show clear working:

  1. Fractions & Money: Layla has £36. She spends 2⁄9 of it on a game. How much money does she have left?
    Answer: £28
    2⁄9 of £36 = 36 ÷ 9 × 2 = £8 spent. £36 − £8 = £28 left.
  2. Ratio & Percentages: A fruit bowl has apples and oranges in the ratio 2:3. There are 30 pieces of fruit altogether. What percentage of the fruit are apples?
    Answer: 40%
    2 + 3 = 5 parts, so each part = 30 ÷ 5 = 6. Apples = 2 × 6 = 12. Percentage: 12 ÷ 30 × 100 = 40%.
  3. Geometry & Measurement: A rectangular playground is 650 cm long and 400 cm wide. Find its perimeter in metres.
    Answer: 21 m
    650 cm = 6.5 m and 400 cm = 4 m. Perimeter = 2 × (6.5 + 4) = 2 × 10.5 = 21 m.
  4. Time & Data: A chart shows laps swum each day: Monday 45, Tuesday 60, Wednesday 51. Swimming club runs for 3 hours each day. What was the average number of laps swum per hour on Tuesday?
    Answer: 20 laps per hour
    60 laps ÷ 3 hours = 20 laps per hour.
  5. Money & Percentages: A bike costs £150. In a sale it is reduced by 20%, then a further £5 is taken off for a loyalty card. What is the final price?
    Answer: £115
    20% of £150 = £30, so the sale price is £150 − £30 = £120. Then £120 − £5 = £115.

English

~20–25 minutes
Part 1 — Week 1 SPaG & Vocabulary Review
  1. Choose the grammatically correct sentence:
    A) Me and him went to the shop.    B) He and I went to the shop.    C) Him and me went to the shop.
    Answer: B) He and I went to the shop.
    Use subject pronouns (he, I) before the verb, not object pronouns (him, me).
  2. Add the missing punctuation:
    we should leave now james said its getting late
    Corrected: “We should leave now,” James said. “It's getting late.”
    Speech marks surround spoken words; a comma sits before the closing speech marks when speech continues; “It's” needs an apostrophe (it is).
  3. Choose the correct spelling:
    A) definately    B) definitely    C) definitly
    Answer: B) definitely
    Remember: “definite” + “ly” — there is no “a” in the middle.
  4. Choose a synonym for enormous:
    A) tiny    B) huge    C) quiet
    Answer: B) huge
    A synonym has a similar meaning. Huge ≈ enormous.
  5. Choose an antonym for ancient:
    A) old    B) ruined    C) modern
    Answer: C) modern
    An antonym has the opposite meaning. Modern ≠ ancient.
Part 2 — Comprehension

Read this passage carefully, then answer the questions:

Zara pressed her face against the cold window, watching the storm rage outside. Branches clawed at the glass, and the sky flashed silver every few seconds. Then she noticed something small and white struggling in the flowerbed below — a barn owl, its wing pinned awkwardly beneath a fallen branch. Without stopping to grab her coat, Zara dashed into the garden, rain stinging her cheeks like tiny needles. Carefully, she lifted the branch and gathered the trembling bird into an old towel. The owl's amber eyes blinked up at her, wide with fear. Back inside, Zara's mother examined the wing and declared it merely bruised, not broken. They kept the owl warm in a box lined with straw until the storm passed. At dawn, Zara carried it to the garden and opened the towel. For a moment the owl simply sat there. Then, with a single powerful beat of its wings, it rose silently into the pale morning sky.
  1. What did Zara find in the flowerbed?
    Answer: A barn owl with its wing pinned under a fallen branch.
    This is stated directly: “a barn owl, its wing pinned awkwardly beneath a fallen branch.”
  2. Why did Zara not stop to grab her coat? What does this suggest about how she was feeling?
    Example answer: She was in too much of a hurry to help the owl — this suggests she was worried about it and cared more about rescuing it quickly than staying dry or comfortable.
  3. What does the word “trembling” tell you about how the owl was feeling?
    Answer: It tells us the owl was frightened.
    “Trembling” means shaking, which usually happens when a creature is scared or in shock.
  4. The writer describes the rain “stinging her cheeks like tiny needles.” What effect does this simile have on the reader?
    Example answer: The simile helps the reader imagine how sharp and painful the rain felt, showing how determined Zara was to help the owl despite the harsh weather.
  5. How do you think Zara felt when she watched the owl fly away? Use evidence from the text to support your answer.
    Example answer: Zara probably felt proud and relieved, because the owl “rose silently into the pale morning sky,” showing it had fully recovered thanks to her care.
Part 3 — Writing Task
Choose ONE of the following prompts:

Option A (Narrative): Write a story that begins with the sentence: “The door creaked open, and nobody was inside.”

Option B (Descriptive): Describe a bustling market on a busy Saturday morning. Use your senses to bring the scene to life.

Write 8–10 sentences for your chosen option. Try to use:
  • Varied sentence openers (not every sentence starting the same way)
  • At least one simile or metaphor
  • Powerful verbs and adjectives
  • Correct punctuation throughout
Example answer (Option B — Descriptive):
The market burst with colour and noise from every direction. Stallholders bellowed their prices over the chatter of the crowd, while the sweet smell of fresh bread drifted through the air. Crates of ruby-red strawberries and golden lemons were piled high like tiny mountains. Somewhere a fiddler played a lively tune, and children darted between legs, chasing a runaway balloon. Steam curled up from a food stall, carrying the spicy scent of grilled onions. Coins clinked as customers haggled cheerfully over prices. By midday, the cobbled square was a sea of shoppers, baskets swinging, voices rising and falling like waves.

Check your own writing for: a clear structure with varied openers, at least one simile or metaphor, ambitious vocabulary, and accurate punctuation throughout.
